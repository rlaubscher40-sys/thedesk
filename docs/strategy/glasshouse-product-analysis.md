# Glasshouse, the product

Date: 7 September 2026
Companion to `glasshouse-competitive-analysis.md`, which covers the Instagram
account. This one is about the thing the account exists to sell.

**On sourcing.** heyglasshouse.com is unreachable from the environment this was
written in, so everything below comes from search results, store listings and
their own marketing copy as it appears in those. Anything inferred rather than
observed is marked. Nothing here should be treated as confirmed without a look
at the live app.

---

## 1. What it actually is

A phone app that identifies the property in front of you and returns everything
known about it, before you have reached the letterbox.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Founder | Elliot Isles (appears to be operating solo or near-solo) |
| Platform | iPhone, plus a Google Play listing under `com.linearuk.app.glasshouse` |
| Interaction | Point the camera at a building; it resolves which one and looks it up |
| Coverage | 5M+ Australian properties, sourced from Valuer General records |
| Expansion | New Zealand, described as fully mapped |
| Model | 3-day free trial into a subscription |

What it returns per property: full sale history with dates and prices, beds /
baths / cars, land and floor area, year built, council capital and land values,
current estimate, zoning, flood risk, school catchments, nearby development
applications, rental data, ten-year trends, and every comparable sale within
2km filtered by timeframe.

Then — and this is the part worth noticing — **it writes you a verdict.**

## 2. The two things that make it work

**A data licence.** Valuer General records, comparables, rental data. This is
bought, not scraped, and it is the entire moat. Everything the app does sits on
top of it.

**An LLM verdict layer.** The raw data is a table; the product is the sentence
that tells you what the table means. That is the same technique The Desk uses on
its own numbers — computed facts, model-written framing.

The instructive part is the combination. Neither half is rare. A data licence
without a verdict is a portal nobody opens twice; a verdict without proprietary
data is commentary. Together they are a product.

## 3. Where it is weak, and it is a real weakness

**It is a utility, not a habit.**

You open Glasshouse when you are standing in front of a property. That is
high-intent and genuinely useful, and it is also *episodic*. Someone buying a
house uses it intensely for six weeks and then has no reason to open it again.
An agent uses it at open homes, so weekly at best.

A subscription needs a reason to still be there in month four. An episodic tool
has to keep manufacturing that reason from outside itself — which is precisely
what a 57K-follower Instagram account is: the frequency the product does not
generate on its own.

**Read their content in that light and it reads differently.** It is not a
marketing channel bolted onto a product. It is doing load-bearing retention work
the app cannot do, which is why it is as good as it is.

## 4. What that means for The Desk

We have been treating this as "they are beating us at content". The more useful
frame is that we are playing different games, and ours has the better shape in
one specific respect.

|  | Glasshouse | The Desk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moat | Licensed transaction data. Real, expensive, ours-can-never-match | A basket of public series tracked daily, and the editorial layer on it |
| Frequency | Episodic. Opened when standing at a property | Daily by construction. The product *is* a habit |
| Content's job | Manufacture the frequency the product lacks | Deliver the product |
| Conversion | Install, then trial, then subscribe | Read, then subscribe |

**Frequency is native to us and bolted on for them.** Every post we make is the
product; every post they make is an advertisement for a product you use
elsewhere. That is a structurally cheaper position to run and a stickier one to
subscribe to — which is worth remembering, because from the outside their
account simply looks like it is winning.

**Their moat is genuinely better than ours.** We should stop pretending
otherwise. Transaction data is worth paying for and public series are not. Our
answer is not to match it but to be the only place that reads the whole basket
together over time, which is what "The Month in Numbers" is for.

## 5. The threat worth actually watching

Not their Instagram. **A daily or weekly product from Glasshouse built on their
own data.**

They have the moat and are visibly learning the content muscle. If they point a
recurring publication at the transaction data — "this week's biggest price cuts,
by suburb", which they can compute and we cannot — they would have both the
proprietary substrate and the frequency, and the position in section 4 stops
being ours.

Their existing posts are already close to this. "$1.66B wiped off Australia's
asking prices. 1,179 drops this week, 148 rises, median -6.0%" is a recurring
data franchise computed from their own index. It is one editorial decision away
from being a subscription product rather than a top-of-funnel post.

**The defensible response is speed, not imitation.** Own the macro basket
completely and be established there before they arrive, because that is the half
of the market their data does not cover: they can tell you what a house sold
for, and they cannot tell you what the cash rate, arrears, approvals, migration
and clearance have done together over a decade.

## 6. What not to do

- **Do not build a property lookup.** They have a licence and a head start, and
  the licence is the product.
- **Do not read their Reels as "we need AI video".** The lesson from their
  account is per-post quality and a recurring named franchise, not a technique.
  For a publication whose position is that its numbers are real, generated
  footage sits badly next to them; the animated card does the same job for
  nothing per post.
- **Do not chase their feature list.** Flood risk, school catchments and DAs are
  a buyer's checklist. Our reader is not making one decision about one house,
  they are tracking a market.
