## Corrections

1. Publisher-disclosed commercial content is held before text enters enrichment. Detection is scoped to the verified publisher and rendered component; a sponsor word anywhere in an article is insufficient.
2. Lending-conduct recognition includes “fraudulent loans”, including home/mortgage variants. Original subject, geography, date, evidence length and priority gates remain required.
3. Headline clustering now vetoes conflicting recognised places, figures, reporting months, release days and opposing movement directions. A deterministic reproduction previously collapsed “Sydney housing prices fall as buyers retreat” and the equivalent Melbourne headline into one event; they now remain separate. These are original synthetic regression cases, not claims that a particular live story was suppressed.
4. New published stories retain private hashes of bounded eight-word sequences, the original day and numeric claims in a separate table. Fingerprints are committed with the feed row and survive the enrichment job's original-input cleanup. They contain no stored article prose and are not added to public feed rows.
5. Scheduled discovery receives up to 500 recent visible local-story fingerprints through the existing authenticated ingest endpoint. Selection holds unchanged-evidence repeats with `already-covered-evidence`; the ingest boundary checks again, including repeats within a submitted batch.
6. Suppression requires the same confirmed original publication day, at least four significant shared headline words, no detected event conflict, unchanged numeric claims, at least 80 matching sequence hashes and identical sets of sequence hashes. Changed numbers, amounts/units/signs, later releases, conflicting regions and rewritten reporting remain eligible for normal evaluation. Generated summaries and angles never establish duplication.
7. Fingerprints use the existing 6,000-character article budget, at most 1,000 hashes per article, a 500-record comparison limit and a four-day recent-feed window. A fourteen-day cleanup runs with editorial report retention. Held rows cannot block later news. Historical rows without stored original fingerprints are not retroactively labelled duplicates.

## Verification and limits

The local pass passed 348 tests with 21 database-dependent tests skipped, plus TypeScript. Added regressions cover commercial disclosure isolation, the observed headline/dek failures, distinct regional events, changed data and publication days, persisted fingerprint comparison and pipeline exclusion reasons. Once original articles have been read, within-run clustering also requires unchanged normalised evidence; even a short nonnumeric update remains separate. The isolated MySQL release test verifies fingerprint insertion, survival after enrichment input cleanup, absence of article text from returned history, and HOLD exclusion. Full CI and deployment results are recorded in the pull request.

This is conservative duplicate suppression, not comprehensive semantic event grouping. It preserves heavily rewritten reports and cannot compare old stories whose original evidence was already cleared. The API recheck reduces repeated imports but does not provide an atomic lock across different URLs submitted simultaneously; existing URL claims still protect canonical-URL races. The place vocabulary is bounded, and broader regional entity matching remains imperfect. Publisher-sponsored markup support currently covers the verified realestate.com.au structure only. No additional publisher, source availability guarantee, social post, financial-data series or unrelated deployment setting is included in this pass.

The existing daily coverage review and scheduled independent audits remain necessary to measure missed events, duplicate burden and ranking quality over time. Tests demonstrate these particular protections; they do not establish that the best story always ranks first.
